Fast & Slow Pointers: one speed trick, five jobs

One pointer moves 1 step, the other 2, on the same forward-only structure. That's the whole mechanism — what changes is what you're using it to find. Code lives in 03-fast-slow-pointers/*.js.

Five variants

One trick, applied to different goals and inputs

01-cycle-detection.js

Meet-in-the-cycle, reset-to-head

After slow/fast meet, a pointer from head and the meeting point both step by 1 — they meet again exactly at the cycle's start.

02-middle-of-list.js

Fast runs out first

Fast covers twice the distance. When it runs out of list, slow is provably at the midpoint — no length count needed.

03-cycle-in-implicit-sequence.js

Cycle on a virtual list

nums[i] IS the next pointer. No real list exists, but Floyd's algorithm doesn't need one — any deterministic "next" rule works.

04-cycle-length-and-alt-start.js

Cycle length, then fixed-gap

Walking from the meeting node back to itself counts one lap. With L known, the fixed-gap technique relocates the start — a second proof of variant 1's result.

05-middle-plus-reverse.js

Fast/slow as a building block

Find the middle (this pattern), reverse the back half (relinking, not swapping), then walk both halves together comparing values.
